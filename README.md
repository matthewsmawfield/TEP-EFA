## Abstract






Twelve Earth gravity assist flybys spanning nine spacecraft are analyzed within the Temporal Equivalence Principle (TEP) framework. TEP posits that proper time is governed by a dynamical scalar field φ; all non-gravitational matter couples universally to a causal matter metric through the conformal factor A(φ) = exp(β_A φ/M_Pl) with β_A = −1. Under Paper 0's amplitude–shear split (v0.15), the Earth-vicinity Temporal Shear is strongly screened by the two-body kinetic operator, so the flyby anomaly cannot be a shear-force effect. The candidate mechanism is instead the clock sector: highly asymmetric, fast hyperbolic flybys accumulate rapid proper-time offsets at perigee through the unsuppressed amplitude channel S_A, which orbit-determination codes — reconstructing velocities under an assumed GR time standard — misread as ΔV discontinuities wherever the tracking link references the spacecraft's own time standard. The channel analysis performed here shows the term survives only in clock-carrying observables (one-way, non-coherent, or regenerative-ranging links and onboard-clock telemetry); in the coherent-transponder class assigned to the published catalogue from the literature, the transponder endpoint cancels it identically, so on that classification the catalogue anomalies are not produced by the clock sector in the measured observable (encounter-specific tracking modes remain to be checked) — the mechanism's claim is instead a falsifiable clock-carrying prediction whose m/s-scale raw amplitude is conditional on the adopted profile and the eccentric-orbit clock constraint in Section 5.7a. The fitted response profile S_⊕(r) is accordingly reinterpreted as the clock-sector amplitude calibration, with the characteristic terrestrial field scale R_sol = R_T(M_⊕) = (3M_⊕/4πρ_T)¹/³ ≈ 4146 km for ρ_T ≈ 20 g/cm³. Since the UCD calibration of ρ_T descends from the GNSS coherence-length measurement, this terrestrial length scale is a single shared input rather than an independent validation. The accumulated proper-time offset manifests as an apparent velocity discontinuity in GR-based orbit reconstruction — a clock-sector artefact misread as ΔV without an unmodeled force — in the measurement classes that carry the spacecraft's own time standard. The non-radial component of the offset integral, modulated by Earth's oblateness (J2, J3, J4), trajectory asymmetry, the velocity-dependent response template, and the perigee plasma template layer, supplies the geometric template against which the observed flyby anomaly pattern is organized. Six published nonzero entries are retained in the re-transcribed catalogue — four firm detections (NEAR, Galileo 1990, Rosetta 2005, Galileo 1992) and two marginal ~2σ entries (Cassini, MESSENGER) — alongside three published nulls/bounds and three flybys without public anomaly reports; the re-transcription against the Anderson et al. (2008) primary source corrected several sign, uncertainty, and declination entries (Section 2.3). Under a full-catalog likelihood with a fractional misspecification floor — a per-flyby systematic set to the measured between-flyby amplitude heterogeneity (CV_β ≈ 0.47) times each model's own predicted signal, iterated to a fixed point and invariant under the arbitrary field-normalization anchor — the archived information-criterion comparison describes how the restricted geometric template organizes the catalogue relative to the Null by Δ BIC ≈ 1.67 × 10⁴; the Anderson empirical baseline, whose two parameters are fitted on the same catalogue, attains a nominally smaller BIC but at parity-level separation (Δ BIC ≈ 4.8), an ordering that reverses in TEP restricted's favour (Δ BIC ≈ +2767) when the published uncertainties are taken at face value without a systematic floor. These scores describe empirical geometry-template organization, not evidence for the clock-sector mechanism, which is absent from the coherent catalogue observable. They retain the archived iteratively weighted fitting convention; a fully optimized prediction-dependent-variance likelihood and its nuisance accounting are required before interpreting them as calibrated model-selection evidence. A structural consistency check is provided by the ordering of anomaly magnitudes against the trajectory-asymmetry factor (cosδ_in − cosδ_out). In the gated n = 6 detection ensemble the ordering is positive but not decisive (Spearman ρ = +0.66, p = 0.156): NEAR carries both the largest asymmetry factor and the largest published anomaly in every ranked subset. Across the broader catalogue the rank association is moderate: Spearman ρ = 0.56 (p = 0.11) over the n = 9 flybys with published asymmetries, and ρ = 0.68 (p = 0.090) over the n = 7 flybys with nonzero published anomalies, computed in the pipeline's ordering diagnostic. These are reported as a qualitative consistency check rather than a primary discriminator. The inverse-variance weighted mean β_fit ≈ 4.74 × 10⁻³ (formal SE 5.83 × 10⁻⁴; random-effects SE 1.28 × 10⁻³; between-flyby τ ≈ 2.23 × 10⁻³) quantifies the cross-flyby effective response scale; it is not the bare conformal coupling β_A. The amplitude-informative per-flyby fits span 4.43 × 10⁻³ to 1.44 × 10⁻² (with the negative-branch Galileo 1992 member at 6.7 × 10⁻²), consistent with geometry-dependent modulation. The fitted amplitudes are trajectory-response coefficients, not the Cassini source charge; Cassini PPN compliance is evaluated through the solar source-charge projection α_eff = S_Σ α_0 (Section 4.6.1a). The re-transcribed catalogue resolves the historical Cassini sign question: the primary-source anomaly is negative (−2 ± 1 mm/s), so the kernel's predicted sign agrees and the residual tension is magnitude-only. The geometry envelope carries seven nominal deterministic coefficients plus one fitted amplitude against six retained nonzero entries, so the model-selection comparisons are read as organisational support under the adopted convention rather than calibrated evidence. This work shows that a restricted trajectory-geometry template quantitatively organizes the published Earth flyby anomaly catalogue under the adopted Anderson trajectory-geometry convention, while remaining consistent with precision solar system constraints and identifying specific stress tests—principally the Galileo 1992 amplitude shortfall and the Juno raw-tension null, together with raw DSN reanalysis—that are required to advance from model organisation to decisive confirmation. The catalogue's evidential status is conditional on one provenance question carried openly here: the orbit-determination filtering interpretation invoked for the published nulls is symmetric with the alternative that the early anomalies were artefacts of less sophisticated orbit-determination pipelines — the two readings are equally consistent with the present data, and only an independent minimal-OD reanalysis of anomaly and null flybys under identical force models can arbitrate between them. The terrestrial response profile S_⊕(r) constitutes the Earth-vicinity geometric realization of the clock-sector amplitude response S_A(E). Rather than a hard boundary, this radial profile traces the continuous amplitude structure of the temporal field within Earth's local potential well.


## Key Findings

Six gated Step 008 fits (NEAR, Galileo 1990, Rosetta 2005, Cassini, Galileo 1992, MESSENGER), all sign-consistent at β<sub>ref</sub> on the re-transcribed catalogue, with amplitude-informative β<sub>fit</sub> spanning 4.43 × 10⁻³ to 1.44 × 10⁻² (Galileo 1992 at 6.65 × 10⁻²), inverse-variance weighted mean β<sub>fit</sub> = 4.74 × 10⁻³ ± 1.28 × 10⁻³ (random-effects SE; formal 5.83 × 10⁻⁴), with the hierarchical Bayesian posterior median β<sub>fit,0</sub> = 5.27 × 10⁻³ (Step 015) reconciling on the same O(10⁻³) amplitude. All gated screened couplings satisfy |γ − 1| ≈ 2β<sub>A,eff</sub>² < 2.3 × 10⁻⁵. Step 026 model comparison on the full n = 9 catalog under the anchor-invariant fractional misspecification floor favours TEP restricted over Null by ΔBIC ≈ 1.67 × 10⁴; the Anderson empirical law fitted on the same catalogue attains a nominally smaller BIC at parity-level separation (ΔBIC ≈ 4.8), an ordering that reverses to ΔBIC ≈ +2767 in TEP restricted's favour under the published-uncertainties-only sensitivity. Step 039 extends the check to the null-result catalog: raw true positives on NEAR, Galileo 1990, and Rosetta 2005, raw true nulls on the published bounds, one deterministic fixed-amplitude raw-tension case (Juno), and a Galileo 1992 amplitude shortfall with correct sign. Step 008 bootstrap (n = 10,000) and leave-one-out stability coefficient ≈ 0.092 on the six-member ensemble.


## Reproduction Steps

The flyby TEP analysis pipeline is fully automated. Run the complete pipeline:

```bash
python scripts/run_all.py
```

This will populate `results/` and `data/processed/` with fresh analysis outputs.
